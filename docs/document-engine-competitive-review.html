The short version. Five things were examined: a benchmark comparing three ways of serving the same small AI model; a teaching demo showing how compressing a model changes its speed and accuracy; a scan of the main public library of open AI models; PaddleOCR, the toolkit our own reading stage is built on; and ai-parsing, a live sibling system solving almost exactly our problem the opposite way.

None is a commercial competitor. All five are useful, because each does something we have written down as important and not yet finished.

Two findings change a decision. We are one model generation behind inside a library we have already installed — the upgrade is a one-line change and claims roughly 5% better reading. And the two open models our architecture document names as strongest are licensed so we cannot ship them to a customer.

01Why this review exists

Snap Apps reads Australian receipts and invoices and turns them into accounting records. The part that does the reading is called the document engine. Its architecture is written down in docs/OCR.md, a long and deliberately honest document whose competitive section opens with a table of how well rival systems perform — and then admits, in bold, that every number in that table is someone else's.

Five links were put forward for comparison. This document answers the question properly: what are they, what do we have, what is planned, and where does the comparison actually bite.

In plain terms

Reading a receipt with a computer sounds simple and is not. The difficulty is not reading the letters — it is knowing, afterwards, whether you read them correctly. A system that is confidently wrong about a tax figure is worse than one that says "I could not read this." Almost everything below is about that distinction.

Note on category

The first two are small personal or academic projects — one has two commits and no stars. Nothing below treats them as rivals. They are treated as evidence, because a small project that has actually measured something outranks a large document that has not.

The last two are different in kind and the most useful of the five. One is code we already ship. The other is a system built by the same people, to the same brief, on the opposite principle — which makes it the nearest thing to a controlled experiment this review contains.

02Where the Snap Apps engine stands today

Before comparing anything, an honest statement of our own position. The engine has three layers, and they are at three very different stages of completion.

CapabilityStatusWhat exists in the codebaseWhat that means in practice
Reading the document Live A vision-language model is handed the whole page and asked for structured data. Default gemma4:31b; a router escalates through four models on failure. Works. But the model never reports where on the page it found a number — it goes straight from picture to answer.
Checking the reading Live Arithmetic, date and ABN checks in validators.ts, including the modulus-89 ABN checksum and a dual-convention line-total check. This is our strongest asset and it is fully built. Deterministic code, not a model, decides whether a human must look.
Audit trail & replay Live Migration 0009: re-extraction updates in place, both runs survive, human corrections are locked, documents behind a posted transaction are frozen. A past result can be reproduced and every correction is traceable. Rare in this field.
Data isolation Live Row-level security in the database; the background worker itself goes through it rather than around it. One tenant cannot read another's documents, enforced by the database, not by application discipline.
Our own OCR stage Shadow packages/docai plus a Python service wrapping PP-OCRv5. Built, tested, and wired in — but only in shadow mode. It runs alongside the real result and never affects it. If the switch is unset, it is not called at all.
Location of every value Shadow grounding.ts attaches coordinates to extracted fields — advisory only. Cannot yet be relied on, because the stage that produces coordinates is not the primary reader.
Deployment restrictions Live registry.ts models residency as a type. An air-gapped profile does not forbid external engines — it removes them from the list. A misconfigured flag cannot send Australian tax records offshore, because the option does not exist in that build.
Calibrated confidence Gap The engine self-reports calibrated: false. The cutoff of 0.8 is labelled in code as a placeholder. When the system says it is 95% sure, that number does not yet mean anything measurable.
Evaluation corpus Gap Four documents, all synthetic, rendered from HTML templates in the bench folder. The manifest says so explicitly. Good enough to exercise the harness. Not good enough to claim accuracy on real Australian paperwork.
Status as of 12 September 2026. "Shadow" means the code runs but its output is stored separately and never changes what the user sees.
The single most important line in this table: the OCR stage is built but not switched on. Every stored layout record from the most recent run reads ppocr-v5 failed: docai-engine sidecar not reachable. Shadow mode that never executes collects no evidence, and the calibration work planned next depends entirely on that evidence.

Our harness is the more sophisticated instrument. Their discipline is the thing worth taking: hold everything constant, change exactly one thing, publish the number you measured. On the one dimension they chose, they have data and we have none.

Why the serving layer is not an idle question for us

Our OCR service is Python and FastAPI — their Stack A. It currently runs with a CPU accelerator switched off, because PaddlePaddle 3.3.1 throws an error on both detection models with it enabled. The workaround is documented and correct. It also means the stage is running slower than its ceiling, and we have never measured what a different serving architecture would recover.

In plain terms

Two parts of the system could be responsible for slowness: the AI itself, or the plumbing around it. We have measured the AI. We have never measured the plumbing. This experiment is a template for doing that in about a day.

04Reference two — the quantization demo

utmcs_llmv is a university teaching tool, deployed 6 September 2026, with two commits and no stars. It demonstrates quantization: compressing an AI model so it is smaller and faster, at some cost to accuracy. It runs one model — Llama 3.2 1B — at three compression levels and lets a student watch the trade-off.

In plain terms

Quantization is like saving a photo as a smaller JPEG. The file shrinks and loads faster; at some point the picture starts to look wrong. The skill is knowing where that point is. For AI models it matters enormously, because the difference between compression levels can be the difference between needing an expensive GPU and not.

Their architecture, and ours

Concernutmcs_llmvSnap Apps
Front endNext.js, TypeScriptNext.js web, React Native mobile
Back endRust / AxumNode / Fastify, plus a Python OCR service
StoreSQLite, append-onlyPostgreSQL with row-level security, append-only edit history
Where AI runsA Mac on the desk, reached over a private networkHosted models today; our own CPU stage in shadow
Separation principleThe web server never runs a model — it stores results and proxies when the Mac is awakeThe same idea, expressed as a type: a deployment profile filters the engine list, so a forbidden engine is absent rather than disabled

Where we are ahead

Ours is the stronger design. Their separation is a network arrangement that can be misconfigured; ours is enforced in code — permitted() removes an engine from the list entirely. Their store has no audit trail, no locked human corrections, no tenant isolation. Ours has all three, tested against a real database.

Where they are ahead

Theirs is running. Ours is behind an unset environment variable, and every stored record says the service was unreachable. A split-brain design that has never completed a run has not been proven — it has been described.

The gap this exposes

Our tier table names "an open AI model on our own GPU" as tier 2 and never pins which model, at which compression. GPU cost is the largest number in the plan's cost section. Meanwhile, in our engine registry, the field recording how long each engine takes reads:

Finding two — the two strongest models are ones we cannot ship

Our architecture document singles out two open systems by name: Chandra 2 as the "best single published number", and Surya 2 as the "best cost/accuracy of the small models". Both are OpenRAIL-licensed. Our own licence rule — written into code as a function that scans the engine list and returns violations — would reject both from any package sent to a customer.

In plain terms

A licence is the legal permission attached to a model. Some open licences let you use a model freely but not redistribute it inside a product you sell. OpenRAIL is that kind. For a consumer app that calls a model over the internet it rarely matters. For an enterprise customer who wants the system installed inside their own building, it decides whether the product can exist at all.

Our own document anticipated this in general — it warns that discovering a licence problem during a customer's procurement review is "a bad day that CI can have instead." It just never named which two models the warning applies to.

The comparison table in docs/OCR.md §2 currently reads as a menu of options. Two of its headline entries are not on the menu. A licence column should be added, so nobody plans around a model we cannot deliver.

06Reference four — PaddleOCR, our own supplier

PaddleOCR is the most widely used open text-recognition toolkit in the world: 89,400 stars, Apache-2.0, supporting over 100 languages. It is not a competitor in the ordinary sense. We ship it. Our dependency file pins paddleocr==3.7.0, and our OCR service is a wrapper around it.

Checking it produced the most immediately actionable finding in this review.

Three consequences for us

ConsequenceDetail
1 Our weakest measured number is the one the upgrade targets Our OCR stage found 7 of 8 text regions — a detection recall of 0.875. Detection is precisely what the new generation claims to improve by 4.6%. This is testable against the benchmark we already have.
2 There may be a way around our disabled accelerator Our service runs with its CPU accelerator switched off, because of a crash in one specific acceleration library. PaddleOCR's 5.2× speed claim is quoted for OpenVINO — a different accelerator entirely, which we have never tried. The workaround we documented may have an alternative we never looked for.
3 The phone tier now has an obvious candidate Phase 1b plans the reading stage running offline on a phone and names no model. A 1.5M-parameter tier with a published Apple-silicon speed figure is exactly that shape. Our code currently offers only two options, mobile and server, both pointing at the older generation.

The uncomfortable half: PaddleOCR is also a competitor

The same project publishes PaddleOCR-VL, a document-parsing model claiming 96.3% on a major public benchmark — and it is Apache-2.0, so unlike Chandra and Surya we could ship it. That model competes with our entire reading stage, not just part of it.

This is not a threat, provided we are honest about where we differ. Our architecture document already says it plainly: do not compete on turning pages into text — use the people who are winning that race. Our differentiation is knowing when we are wrong, proving where every number came from, and being deployable where the internet is forbidden. None of those are things PaddleOCR-VL offers, and none of them are reading accuracy.

The one genuinely measured quantity in that system is the deterministic one. Its arithmetic cross-check caught a line total of $518.00 against a stated $512.00, and $760.00 typed against $700.00 struck through by hand. Cheap arithmetic found what a self-reported 95% confidence did not.
Why this matters more than the other four references

This is a live system, built to the same brief, that demonstrates the exact failure our architecture is designed to prevent — and demonstrates it while reporting high confidence. It is the strongest evidence in this review for our central design choice, and it did not come from a benchmark or a vendor claim. It came from reading a sibling system's source.

It also shows the cost of the opposite choice honestly: the parts of ai-parsing that worked are the parts that were arithmetic, and the part that failed is the part that asked a model to police itself.

The warning we should take personally

Two of the four problems recorded against ai-parsing are not really about reading documents at all. One is that a decision written down as a rule was never implemented as one. The other is that access control was written permissively and never tightened.

Our own repository closed a defect of precisely that shape on the day this review was written. The commit message begins: "Two decisions that were written down, believed, and never applied." A sign-in endpoint that was meant never to reach production had its production guard applied to the wrong route; and a model exclusion recorded in our AI document had never been enforced in code.

The pattern is the finding. This engine's strength is that its important decisions are written down with reasons. Its recurring weakness — visible in ai-parsing, in the unenforced exclusion, in the sign-in guard, and in the OCR stage sitting behind an unset switch — is that writing a decision down is being mistaken for shipping it.

09What this changes

Six actions, in the order they should be taken. Four are hours of work; two are days.

ActionEffortFromWhy
1 Start the OCR service and complete one real shadow run Hours Our own state Every stored layout says the service was unreachable. Nothing downstream — calibration, grounding, the Phase 1 promotion — can proceed without evidence from a run that actually happened.
2 Point the sidecar at the newer model generation Hours PaddleOCR PP-OCRv6 ships inside the version we already pin. Same licence, same cost, claimed +4.6% detection — which is our weakest measured number. A one-line change, then re-run the benchmark that already exists.
3 Add a licence column to the competitive table Hours Model library The two models named as strongest cannot be shipped to a customer. Recording that now prevents a plan being built on them.
4 Fix the character-error-rate reporting Hours Our own state The OCR stage scores 6.6% error strictly and 0.0% ignoring spacing — it reads $1,042.60 as $ 1 , 042.60. That is a spacing convention, not a misreading, and the headline number overstates the error.
5 Try the alternative CPU accelerator Days PaddleOCR Our accelerator is off because of a crash in one library. PaddleOCR's published 5.2× speed figure is for a different one we have never tried. This may make the documented workaround unnecessary.
6 Run the two missing measurements Days Demos 1 & 2 A compression sweep for the tier-2 model, and one serving-layer comparison for the OCR service. Both are gaps the plan does not close; both have an existing harness.
Actions 1, 2 and 4 together would replace most of the uncertain numbers in this document with measured ones, in under a day.

The honest summary

The engine's design is well ahead of the research demos and, on the dimensions an accounting customer actually buys, well ahead of the open models too: provenance, calibrated abstention, deterministic validation, replay, audit trail, residency as a type. Against ai-parsing — the only other system here built to do our job — the gap is not close, and it is a gap in the things that decide whether a finance team can trust the output at all. None of that is aspiration; most of it is built and tested.

What the five references expose is narrower and fairly uncomfortable, and it is the same thing three times over:

The fix is not more architecture. The architecture is genuinely good, and the decisions in it are right — including the one ai-parsing proves by getting wrong. The fix is to close the gap between a decision recorded with its reasons and a decision running in production: switch on the stage that is already written, ask for the models we already downloaded, run it against real documents, and fill in the numbers our own code still marks null.
