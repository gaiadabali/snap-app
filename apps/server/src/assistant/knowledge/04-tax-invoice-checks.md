## What makes a valid tax invoice

To claim a GST credit, the invoice behind the purchase has to follow the ATO's rules. In plain words, a tax invoice shows the words "tax invoice", the seller's ABN and the GST charged. Above a set amount it also has to identify the buyer.

A tax invoice is only needed to claim a GST credit once a purchase reaches a set amount. The exact figures come from the assistant's tax tools, so ask Snapper and it will state them.

A plain till receipt without these details is often not a tax invoice.

## What Snap Apps checks

On every docket it reads, the rules run in plain code, not as a guess from the model:

- the docket is marked as a tax invoice
- the seller's ABN passes the ATO checksum
- the GST is exactly one eleventh of the taxable lines
- the lines add up to the total
- above the set amount, the buyer is identified as well

## When a check fails

You get the reason and a suggested fix, not a bare red cross. A missing ABN, an ABN that fails its checksum or no GST breakdown each names the rule it broke. The fix might be to add the ABN, retake the photo, or ask the supplier for a proper tax invoice. A person decides. The app does not fill the gap for you.

## Frequently asked

### Why can't I claim the GST on this receipt?
A valid tax invoice is needed to claim a GST credit once a purchase reaches a set amount. A docket missing a required detail, most often the supplier's ABN, is flagged with what to ask the supplier for.

### The ABN shows as invalid but I know it is right
Check for a transposed digit from the photo first, because the checksum is exact. If the ABN is correct and still fails, it may have been misread. Correct it on the review screen.
