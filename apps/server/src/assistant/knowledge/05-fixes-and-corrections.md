## A blank rather than a guess

When Snap Apps is not confident about a figure, it leaves the field empty and flags it for you to fill in. A wrong figure is worse than a missing one, because a missing field asks for your attention and a wrong one does not.

## The review screen

The review screen shows the original image beside the fields it read, so you can check a figure against the real docket. A document that needs review shows the specific check that failed, such as a mismatched total, an ABN that does not check out or a date that looks wrong, rather than a generic low-confidence notice.

## Making a correction

Edit any field directly. What the reader originally said is never overwritten. It stays attached to the record beside your corrected value, so there is a full trail of what was read and what it was changed to.

## Your corrections are locked

Once you correct or confirm a field, it is locked. A later re-read can disagree with it, and if it does that is raised for you to look at, but it can never overwrite your edit on its own.

## Rejecting a mis-scan

Photographed the wrong thing? Reject it. The original is retained under the same rules as everything else, but it is taken out of your working list.

## Confidence and flags

Every field carries its own confidence, not one score for the whole document. A crisp total can sit beside a smudged line on the same receipt. If the checks fail on the first read, the document is re-read by a stronger model before it reaches you as needing review.

## Frequently asked

### A field is blank instead of filled in. Why?
The reader leaves a field blank rather than guess when it is not confident. Fill it in on the review screen.

### I fixed a field. Could a re-read change it back?
No. Corrected and confirmed fields are locked against being overwritten by a machine re-run.

### What if I photographed the wrong thing?
Reject it from the review screen. It leaves your working list.
